## Goal

Alpöge's note says a chain of about √X theta lifts plays the role of multiplicity in Stepanov's method, and that charged lift by lift the method stalls at exponent 2/3. Nobody has explained how the code realises this. These notes work from the top theorem downward and record what each layer actually states.

## 2. The √X chain length is a hypothesis of the bound

`core_gm` needs `CoreAtH`, defined in `Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/PrimitiveCosets/CoreAtH.lean:29`. It packages a record `Dd : Data U0 W Top m f` plus size and window conditions. One of them is `RestSize`, `Formalism/SizeWindowBrackets/SChain.lean:53`, which includes

```
x ^ (1/2 − ε) ≤ m        and        k ≤ n² · x ^ (1/2 − ε)
```

so the parameter `m` must be at least about √x. Verified. That `m` is the length of the chain of theta lifts is **Reading**: in `Data` (`Formalism/RankGapBounds/MeanBoundSecondForm.lean`), `m` indexes per-step weights `w` and energies `EeJ`, and the energy is their sum over j < m, which fits one term per lift, but the record itself is just real numbers.

The same block requires a "move cost" of at most x^(1/2 + ε) (`XLarge`, same file). Verified.

## 3. The layers so far

1. **Endgame** (verified): square-root bound on Gm ⇒ Landau ⇒ no zeros ⇒ `EveryCharacter` ⇒ RH.
2. **Inequality layer** (verified as a shape): `Data` is a bookkeeping record of real numbers. `SizeData₂` (`SChain.lean`) bundles its hypotheses: `HidaSetting`, `DIV`, `EComp5`, `HComp`, `Count5`, `Slopes`, `QuotientSlopeBoundsWith`, `LossBounds`, `SlopeIneq`, `HHBound`, `FParams`. 3. **Tie to arithmetic** (located, not yet read): `ChainTied`, `Formalism/SharpRestriction/GlobalSchurBounds/TieGlobal.lean:97`, attaches the abstract `Data` to a family `FamP` of arithmetic objects through `TiePts`, `TieGlobal`, `TieLocal` and `TieArith`. The family's host carries two primes `q` and `d`, matching the `WinPair` conditions (q ≡ 3 mod 8, d ≡ 7 mod 16) in the window bound. This is where theta lifts, Hida theory and divisibility should enter.

## 5. Where √X comes from: the balance, verified line by line

The whole square-root saving is one inequality, `proposition_size_bound` (`Formalism/SizeWindowBrackets/ChainSlopeWindows/LipschitzWeightAndMinimum.lean:507`, Exact: used). It proves `GrowthSizeBound`:

```
nT·m·b·(δ₂/2)·(G(x) − 4x^(1/2+ε))  ≤  Err + 2·nT·m·b/x        (main term on the left)
Err ≤ x^(Cε)·[ nT·aE·(1 + m²/k)  +  m·(2n·m·b·x^ε)  +  m·(nT2·k·b·x^ε / (n²·x^(−ε))) ]
⟹  G(x) ≤ x^(1/2 + (C+3)ε)
```

Here G = `Gz`, the ζ-part of the explicit formula (section 1), m is the chain length, n = |H| the size of the character group, nT and nT2 are counts of "top" and "thick" points, b and aE ≈ x·b are scales, and k is a second size parameter. The constraints come from `SizeWindows` / `RestSize` (same files):

| Constraint | Used for |
|---|---|
| aE ≤ 2xb | |
| m ≥ x^(1/2 − ε) | term 1 |
| m ≤ x^(1/2 + ε/2) | term 3 |
| x·m ≤ k ≤ n²·x^(1/2 − ε) | terms 2 and 4 |
| nT ≥ x^(−ε/8)·n/2, nT2/n ≥ x^(−ε) | dividing out |

Dividing each error term by the main-term size nT·m·b gives four lemmas in the same file (`SZ.T1`–`SZ.T4`, verified):

| Term | After dividing by nT·m·b | Bound | Lemma |
|---|---|---|---|
| nT·aE | aE/(m·b) ≈ **x/m** | ≤ x^(1/2+ε), needs m ≥ √x | `T1` |
| nT·aE·m²/k | x·m/k | ≤ 2, needs k ≥ x·m | `T2` |
| m·(2n·m·b·x^ε) | 2n·m/nT ≈ **m** | ≤ x^(1/2+…), needs m ≤ √x | `T3` |
| m·(nT2·k·b·…) | k/(n·nT) | ≤ x^(1/2+…), needs k ≤ n²√x | `T4` |

So the error, relative to the main term, is about **x/m + m**, and both are √x exactly when m ≍ √x. The lower bound m ≥ x^(1/2−ε) and upper bound m ≤ x^(1/2+ε/2) pin the chain length to √x from both sides. Verified.

**Where 2/3 would come from (Reading, checked by arithmetic).** The m-term is m·B0 with B0 ≤ 2n·m·b·x^ε (`BNBounds`, `Formalism/SizeWindowBrackets/SChain.lean:34`, Exact: used). So the total chain cost is quadratic in m, and per unit of main term it is linear in m. If that cost were one power of m worse (cubic total, for example each of the m lifts paying an O(m²) integrality cost on its own), the balance would be x/m + m², minimised at m = x^(1/3) with value x^(2/3). That matches the outline's statement that charging lifts separately stalls at 2/3. The code does not mention 2/3 anywhere on the proof path; this is my reconstruction of the counterfactual.

**Correction (checked after writing the line above).** B0 is not a free quantity that must be shown small. It is *defined* as a budget: `SizeData₂.B0 := sizeBudget D.R x ε = f·m·b·x^ε` (`Formalism/LocalRingCardinality/DataParams₂.lean`, with `sizeBudget` in `DataArith.lean:200`; f = `R.f`, b = exp(x^ε)). So `BNBounds` is bookkeeping (it should reduce to f ≤ 2|H|; not yet checked). The content is in the statements that real quantities **fit inside** this budget, chiefly the per-lift energy condition `EComp` (section 6), which the family proof gets from `LinkDIV_at` (`Formalism/SharpRestriction/GlobalTheoremInputs.lean`):

```
for every word u and every lift j = 1..m:
  | Σ_w sz(w, E(u,j,w))  −  (Σ_t aT t)·log(g₀·g₁) |  ≤  C·B0 = C·f·m·b·x^ε
```

The budget per lift grows linearly in m. Summed over m lifts that is the m² total cost of section 5.

## 6. The slope layer: where the "same slope cancels"

The per-point bound used by the proof is `each_unit_bound_statement` (`…/ChainSlopeWindows/LevelOfCongruence.lean:175`, Exact: used), which proves `PointwiseBoundVariant`: ASK(u0) ≤ Main(u0) + `ErrX5at`(u0). (The averaged version `MeanBoundWith`/`errorTermWith` exists in the source but is **not** used; Exact.) Its hypotheses, bundled as `SizeData₂`:

- `EComp5`: |Ee(u0,u) − m·sumA·lg| ≤ cE·m·B0. The chain energy Ee is m copies of one step's slope (sumA·lg, with lg = log(g₀g₁) from the host, `TieGlobal`), up to error m·B0. This is where "tracking divisibility along the chain" has to land: the total energy is linear in m with an error controlled by B0.
- `QuotientSlopeBoundsWith`: the maximal slope of the quotient lattice N is at most that of M, plus gain Ga and Fo, minus Ee, plus cSl1·m·B0; and a matching lower bound. These are Bost-style slope inequalities: `muMaxN`, `muMaxM`, `muN`, `muM` are tied by `TieLocal` to `(Fm.D x u0).div.Nu u` (rank, μ, μ_max) and `((Fm.D x u0).fm u).lat 0`.
- `Count5`, `Slopes`, `DIV`, `HComp`, `LossBounds`, `SlopeIneq`, `HHBound`, `FParams`: counting, divisibility and loss bookkeeping.

The cancellation Whidden described is visible: `Ga_bound5` (line 113) combines `s_lower5`, which uses the **upper** bound Ee ≤ m(sumA·lg + cE·B0), with `key_identity5` (line 105), which contains **+m·sumA·lg**. The m·sumA·lg terms cancel, leaving only the m·cE·B0 error. The proof of `Ga_bound5` ends in `linarith`. Verified. As he said, the cancellation is routine; the content is in proving `EComp5` and `QuotientSlopeBoundsWith` for the real family with B0 this small.

## 8. What the local energy `E u j w` is

`E` is a field of `SizeData₂` (`Formalism/LocalRingCardinality/DataParams₂.lean`). The proof pins it down through the condition `SizeData₂.EDef`, part of `ErrDefs` (`Formalism/LocalRingCardinality/DataErr.lean`, both Exact: used):

```
EDef:  for every word u, lift j ∈ 1..m, place w:   E u j w = Eval L X K c u j w
```

(The constructor `SizeData₂.withEval`, which would set E := Eval directly, is not used; Exact.) `Eval` is a case split on the residue characteristic p of the place w. All of the following is Verified (`DataErr.lean`, lines 82–169):

| Place w lies over | E u j w = | Depends on j? |
|---|---|---|
| p = T_u, the auxiliary prime of word u | `EatT`: twice the total weight deviation `Wtau` + f·Nm + (#tops)(t+1)(m+1)/4, if w is "seen" | no |
| p = g₀ or g₁ (the two host primes) | v_p(DGam) + `EatG`: `FTypeG j` + `vNN` + c_g·f·(Nm² + m²·etaSz) | yes |
| p in the ramified class at d | v_p(DGam) + `EatD j` = `FRamD j` + (max over words of Σ_t ctD)/2 | yes |
| small p (residue class zero or "Sg"), p ≤ X'' | v_p(DGam) + `EatC` or `EatI` (log terms, weights wj, wPl, N(j−1)·N(j)) | yes |
| anything else | v_p(DGam) (usually 0) | no |

**The main term lives at g₀ and g₁.** Over those primes,

```
vNN(i,u,j,w) = ( Σ_τ nuAbs(u,j,τ) · v_w( τ(π̄_{g_i}) ) ) / e_w,    nuAbs(u,j,τ) = Σ_{i < N(j−1)} nuRow(u,j,τ,i)
```

where π_{g_i} is an element of the CM field E with π·π̄ = g_i (`ErrX.piG_norm`). *Reading:* summing sz(w, ·) = 2·lw(w)·e_w·(·) over the places w above g_i turns the valuations of π̄_{g_i} into log g_i (a product-formula computation). So the g-place part of Σ_w sz(w, E(u,j,w)) is roughly (Σ_τ nuAbs(u,j,τ))·log(g₀g₁). Comparing with `EComp`, which says Σ_w sz(w, E(u,j,w)) = (Σ_t aT t)·log(g₀g₁) + O(B0), the per-lift condition seems to amount to:

1. **row sums match the top window:** Σ_τ nuAbs(u,j,τ) ≈ Σ_t aT t at every lift j, and
2. **every other place contributes at most O(B0):** the T_u place, the d-ramified places, the small primes, and the correction terms in `EatG`.

Not yet checked against the proof of `EComp` for the family (`data_EComp5` and the `LinkDIV` provers in `Formalism.L2.DIV`).

**Where j enters (Verified):** through N(j−1) and N(j) (the number of rows), `FTypeG j` = −(D⁺_j + D⁻_j)/2, `FRamD j`, and the weights wj(j). The ranks come from `Ranks` (`Formalism/LocalRingCardinality/Params.lean`):

```
N(j)     = k + j·(t+1)          rows at lift j
rankY(j) = r + j·t              lattice rank at lift j   (r = k + m − 1)
sigW(j)  = (k + j − 1, m − j)   a signature at lift j
```

with m ≤ f = 2·ord ≤ k, t odd, 3m < t. *Reading:* a signature that moves from (k, m−1) to (k+m−1, 0) as j goes from 1 to m, with ranks growing linearly, looks like a chain of theta lifts along a tower of unitary groups, one step per lift. Each step adds t+1 rows. `D⁺_j` and `D⁻_j` sum the parameters a⁺, a⁻ over the two parts of the signature (`Formalism/LocalToGlobal/Divisibility/Numbers.lean:189–193`).

## 9. How the per-lift energy condition is proved (all Exact: used)

```

 ⇐ secondComplement_of_split            g-part + off-g part
     g-part: C2atG_of (WeightDegreeSizes.lean:433) from

        TypePrimeSizeBound      (errG(j) + 2v_g(DGam) + 2c_g f(Nm² + m²·etaSz))·log g_i ≤ C·B0
     off-g part: C2off_of from LevelPrimeBudget, StabiliserLogBound,

```

So the product-formula reading in section 8 is confirmed: the g-place energy is *exactly* (Σ_τ nuAbs)·log g_i. `fockDeg D j` = Σ_τ (Σ_a |kaBig j τ a| + Σ_a |kaSmall j τ a|) (`DataForced.lean:119`), and `FTypeG j = −fockDeg(j)/2` cancels it inside `EatG`.

**The row structure (Verified, `nuAbs_place_fine`, `FockDegreeGapBound.lean`).** At lift j = i+1 and archimedean place τ, nuAbs sums N(j−1) = k + i(t+1) rows `nuRow(u,j,τ,r)`:

| Rows | Each row is | Error per row |
|---|---|---|
| r = 0 and r = k−1 (ends) | top weight / off-top weight | 4Sc + 2N(m) + 1 + etaSz + … |
| 1 ≤ r ≤ k−2 (middle) | ≈ wj(j,τ) | Sc + N(m) (`head_row`, `cloud_u`) |
| k ≤ r < N(j−1): **i(t+1) block rows** | ≈ 0 | 2·etaSz (`block_row`, `be_close`) |

with `nuRow(u,j,τ,r) = srcPar(u,τ,r) + wSz(j,τ) − (N(j−1) + N(j) − 1 − 2r)/2` (`DataDIVDefs.lean:70`). *Reading:* a parameter plus a weight minus a ρ-type half-sum looks like the Harish-Chandra parameters of the archimedean component at lift j, and `kerPlace` sums φ(ν) = ν log ν − ν over the rows (a Stirling / log Γ shape). Not confirmed.

**Where the lift index enters:** the block rows. Each lift adds t+1 of them, so the per-lift row-sum error contains i·(t+1)·2·etaSz ≤ m(t+1)·2·etaSz, which grows linearly with the lift index (Verified). This is the term f·m·t·etaSz in `errG`.

**This revises section 5's reading.** The per-lift error `errG(j)` is dominated by 2·Σ_τ wj(j,τ) ≈ 2·f·b (wj ≈ b on thick places), which has no factor m, while the budget is B0 = f·m·b·x^ε. So the energy condition `EComp` has a spare factor of about m·x^ε / log g. It is **not** what forces the m² total. Something else that is charged against B0 (candidates: `DIV`, `HComp`, `QuotientSlopeBoundsWith`, `Lo`, all of which contain m·B0) must need it. Open question.

## 10. The divisibility inequality `DIV`: a height bound for a chain of m maps

`DIV` is the 7th part of `LinkDIV_at`: μ_CC + h(u) − Fo ≤ MAIN(u) + C·nT·a·La + C·m·(B0 + Nc)·x^(Cabs·ε). The used route (all Exact: used):

```
LinkDIV_at  ⇐ linkDIV_at_words (RecBridge.lean:327), 7th part from WordFacts (RecBridge.lean:128)
WordFacts   ⇐ DivClausesR clause 3:   μ_CC − meanLog κ + h_u − 2·Fo  ≤  R.rhs
                (ConstantGeTwentyEight.lean:111), with divClausesR_of (line 184)
R.rhs       =  m·Σ_t (φ(aT) − aT·logOm) − Σ_t aT·log T  +  (50C0+10)·nT·a·La  +  60·ce·m·x^ε·(B0 + Nc)
                (RecNums.rhs, Inputs.lean;  rhs_le_record, RecBridge.lean)
h_u         =  hA + Σ_{j=1..m} (hB(j) − hX(j))          (RecNums.hu)
```

**The chain (Verified, `height_chain_atR`, ConstantGeTwentyEight.lean:123).** For the pairing map B_u, at each archimedean place v:

```
log ‖B_u‖²_v  ≤  hP  +  Σ_{j=1..m} hK(j,v)  −  log κ_v  −  min_p Σ_j log λ_j(p,v)
```

where B_u factors through a column chain `ColChain` of m maps `Kj` (one per lift) with ‖K_j‖²_v ≤ exp(hK(j,v)). This is submultiplicativity of operator norms along the chain: the height of the composite is at most the sum of the heights of the m steps. *Reading:* this is where "a chain of theta lifts" is turned into an inequality.

**The per-lift charge is built in (Verified, `ZscOfNum`, ZscOfNum.lean:63–66; `errB_of_units`, DivisionHeightBoundStatement.lean).** The scale data for the family sets, **per lift**:

## 13. What proves Eb, and how big it really is (check a)

Question from section 11: `Eb` enters `DIV` as a hypothesis of size ce·m·x^ε·(B0 + Nc). Is that size really needed, or is it fed only by the small Iwasawa/Ш loss (`VWinAt`, about √b)?

**Answer: the full budget is used, and both factors of m are structural.** Eb = (number of lifts) × (per-lift charge). The number of lifts is m. The per-lift charge is a sum over small primes of a Selmer colength, and that colength is (rank of H¹_f) × (lift exponent), where the rank is a Bloch–Kato count that is again m. The Ш loss is a separate additive term and is tiny.

**Step 1: the outer m is the number of lifts (Verified).** `errB_of_units` (`Formalism/LocalToGlobal/Divisibility/DivisionHeightBoundStatement.lean:846`, Exact: used) proves m·C·l3u + LG + Σ_j (E1_j + E2_j) + E3 ≤ ce·m·x^ε·(B0 + Nc). The sum over j has |J| = m terms (`RecNums.p12`, `Divisibility/Inputs.lean:116`: card J = m), each charged C·E1u.

**Step 2: E1u is a defined unit; the real obligation is `IndefData.ON` (Verified).** In `ZscOfNum` (`Divisibility/ZscOfNum.lean:59`, Exact: used) the field is set by definition, E1u := x^ε·(B0 + Nc), so the hypothesis `hE1` is reflexive. The actual content is the `ON` field of `IndefSide` (`IndefiniteForms/IndefSide.lean:323`): for each lift j,

```
Σ_{w ∈ Tw} log(w) · (ram_w · err0(w,j) + ram_w · J0(w,j))  ≤  E1_j = C · E1u
```

i.e. the log-weighted lift exponents at the twisted places. On the family side this is `DIVinN_at` / `LinkN_at` (`Formalism/SharpRestriction/GlobalTheoremInputs.lean:59` and `:40`, both Exact: used):

```
etaSum(j) ≤ dZ0(j) · ( C·(f·m·b·L² + log²x·Nc) + Σ_ℓ vvTerm(ℓ) )
```

The Ш loss is the last term, Σ_ℓ vvTerm(ℓ), added once and bounded by √b (`VVle`). The f·m·b·L² term is the one that matters.

**Step 3: lifts do not share local pieces (Verified).** `linkN_of_gd` (`HeckeGaloisCriteria/LinkNOfGd.lean:38`, Exact: used) bounds etaSum(j) through `etaJ_le` (`IsHeckeGaloisIff.lean:73`, Exact: used), which unfolds etaJ(j,w) = Σ_{i ∈ locs j w} etaLoc(i) (`etaJ_tie`). By `NData.locs_mem` (`LevelLetterCocycles/DataN.lean:83`, Exact: used), i ∈ locs j w ↔ j(i) = j ∧ w(i) = w. So every local piece belongs to exactly one lift. There is no m-fold double count across lifts: the m lifts are charged for m disjoint sets of pieces.

**Step 4: the inner m is a Bloch–Kato rank (Verified).** The per-piece allowance at a small prime ℓ is

```
costC(ℓ) = 6000 · f·m·hB·sl(ℓ) + 30000 · f²·t²·nV·abelian₂(ℓ)        (RowDef.lean:11, Exact: used)
hB      = b + Sc + etaSz + t + 2                                     (RowSmall.lean:15, used)
```

so the dominant term is f·m·b per prime (b = exp(x^ε) dominates hB). The colength bound behind it is `rowNum` = locDeg(u)·m·e' + r·c0(u) (`EmbeddingTypes.lean:284`, used), and the m there comes from `length_bound_of_admitted` (`JumpAndLengthOfAdmitted.lean:961`, used) calling `rank_le_row` (`:67`, used) with m := D.R.m:

```
finrank H¹_f(K_w, T)  ≤  locDeg(w) · m
```

This is the Bloch–Kato dimension count: for a crystalline T with H⁰ = 0, dim H¹_f = Σ_τ #{non-negative Hodge–Tate weights}. The hypothesis `hJ` asks for a set Lo of such weights per embedding with Lo.card ≤ m, and the family supplies it by **equality** (`le_of_eq hs.2.2.1`, line 982 of the same file), from its own gain pattern (`admG2`). So the family's pieces have exactly m non-negative weights per embedding, and the rank bound is attained by the hypothesis itself; it is not slack. Summing locDeg over places above ℓ gives f, so the colength is f·m·e', with e' ≈ hB ≈ b.

**Step 5: putting it together (Reading).** Per lift: Σ_{ℓ small} log ℓ · f·m·b·sl(ℓ) ≈ f·m·b·x^ε = B0, plus √b of Ш loss. Over m lifts: m·B0, which is exactly the Eb budget. So:

| Piece of Eb | Source | Size |
|---|---|---|
| number of lifts | card J = m | m |
| rank per piece | Bloch–Kato: #non-negative HT weights = m (by equality in the family) | m·locDeg |
| exponent per piece | lift exponent e' ≈ hB ≈ b = exp(x^ε) | b |
| primes | Σ log ℓ over small primes | ≈ x^ε |
| Ш loss (`VWinAt`) | Σ vvTerm ≤ √b, added once per lift | negligible |

**What this settles.** The `VWinAt` lead (section 12, credits) does not carry the size: Eb is fed by the per-lift Selmer colengths, not the Ш loss. Unlike the signature drift of section 11 (whose signed sum nearly cancels), neither factor of m here looks removable inside this argument: one is the number of lifts, the other is a Hodge–Tate count that the family fixes by equality. Shrinking it would mean a family with fewer non-negative weights per lift, which changes the representations, not a bound. This is consistent with Gz(x) = Ω(√x): Eb ≈ m²·b·x^(2ε) is a genuine m² cost, and it is a natural candidate for the "+m" side of the balance x/m + m.

## 16. The signature drift is avoidable: its signed size is negligible (check b)

Section 11 claimed, by arithmetic only, that the drift term of `DIV` nearly cancels if summed with signs. Checked against the code (every declaration named here is Exact: used):

**1. The drift is exactly 2j − 1 − m (Verified).** In the family record, `k := N.R.k` (`Formalism/LocalToGlobal/Divisibility/NumbersLink.lean:560`) and pq_j = (k + j − 1) − (m − j) (line 574). **2. The absolute value is a pointwise triangle step (Verified).** In `divisor_inequality_assembly` (`…/HermitianTwistDivisors/DivAssembly.lean:11`), the step `hA` bounds each term (pq_j − kk)·w(j,ρ)·logOm by |pq_j − kk|·|w(j,ρ)|·|logOm| **before** summing. Without that one step, the same proof yields the exact signed sum Σ_j Σ_ρ (pq_j − k)·w(j,ρ)·logOm. `TheoremDivIneq` (`StatementK.lean`) then bounds the absolute version crudely, from |pq_j − k| ≤ m, 0 ≤ w ≤ 2b, |logOm| ≤ La, |J| = m and |Pl| ≤ f, giving m·f·m·2b·La, the "≈ 6·m·B0" of section 11.

**3. At each place, the m lift weights are almost equal (Verified).** The weights are w(j,ρ) = `(hc ρ).e (j − 1)` (`Numbers.lean:160`, the same values as `wj` in `DataParams₂.lean:73`). `SizeData₂.be_close` says |b_i − e_j| ≤ η at every place, so **|w(j,ρ) − w(j′,ρ)| ≤ 2η for all lifts j, j′**. Here η = etaSz, and `szRow_eta_bounds` proves **etaSz ≤ b / (t³·f·k³)**.

**4. The signed sum (checked by arithmetic from 1 to 3).** Since Σ_{j=1}^{m} (2j − 1 − m) = 0, subtracting the j = 1 weight changes nothing:

```
Σ_j Σ_ρ (2j−1−m)·w(j,ρ)·logOm  =  logOm · Σ_ρ Σ_j (2j−1−m)·( w(j,ρ) − w(1,ρ) )
|signed drift|  ≤  La · f · (m²/2) · 2η   =  f·m²·η·La
|absolute drift, as bounded in the proof|  ≤  2·f·m²·b·La
ratio  ≤  η / (2b)  ≤  1 / (2·t³·f·k³)  ≤  x^(−1131)        (t ≥ x^377)
```

So the signed drift is smaller than the bound the proof uses by a factor of at least x^1131, and smaller than the m·B0 budget by about the same factor. **The drift's m² is an artefact of taking absolute values term by term.** A version of `DivAssembly` that kept the signed sum would remove it with a two-line change.

**Consequence (Reading).** With section 15, every m·B0 charge in the per-point error except **Eb** is now either slack (energy, slope) or an artefact of bookkeeping (drift). The "+m" side of the balance, the reason the chain is cut at m ≈ √x, rests on one term: **Eb, m lifts × a Bloch–Kato rank of m per piece** (section 13).

## 18. Why the two m's in Eb are the same m, where b enters, and no 2/3 route in the archive

### Why the Bloch–Kato rank per piece is m (Verified, Reading for the last step)

Section 13 found that the rank bound `rank_le_row` is met with |Lo| = m, by equality, from the family's own weight pattern. That pattern is `ShapeJumps k m t` (`Formalism/SharpRestriction/LevelLetterCocycles/EmbeddingTypes.lean:99`, used), required by `admG2` (line 122) at every small prime, place and embedding τ:

```
Hodge–Tate multiplicity of the twisted lifted representation rTwO at τ
   = indicator of   C  ∪  L  ∪  [0, t)          (each weight with multiplicity 1)
   C : exactly k − 1 weights in a far "cloud" s ≤ sgn·a ≤ s + S
   L : exactly m weights near 0 (|a| ≤ η), outside [0, t), not −1 or t
   [0, t) : the t-block, from the cyclotomic twists χ^c, c = 1..t   (ambBlock)
```

The dimensions match the ranks exactly (`Formalism/LocalRingCardinality/Params.lean`): r = k + m − 1 (line 32), nV = r + t (line 34). So `WpO` (dimension r) has weights C ∪ L, of sizes (k − 1, m). That is the **starting signature** of the chain, sigW(0) = (k − 1, m) (line 46). Each lift moves one unit across, sigW(j) = (k + j − 1, m − j), until the definite group at j = m.

**Reading.** The m low weights L are the m-side of the signature, and the chain has exactly m lifts because it has to walk that m-side down to 0. So Eb's m² = (number of lifts) × (Bloch–Kato rank per piece) is **one m used twice, by construction**: a chain from U(k − 1, m) to the definite U(k + m − 1, 0) has length m, and the indefinite part it starts from has m low weights. Making either factor smaller would mean a shorter chain or a smaller indefinite part, i.e. smaller m, which costs on the x/m side.

### Where b enters the per-piece cost (Verified)

The per-piece colength is (rank) × (lift exponent e′). In the family, e′ is **`eL ℓ N h₁`** (`Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/ResidueCharacters/InvariantsKilledOverCyclotomicAlt.lean:15`):

```
eL l N h = 32·(2·h·sN(l, N) + 1) + lamN(l, N, h),      lamN = sN + log_l(2h) + log_l N + 1
h = h₁ = hN = ⌊hB⌋      (RowAtOfSetup.lean:33, LocalErrorBoundPlaces.lean:18)
hB = b + Sc + etaSz + t + 2,     b ≤ hB ≤ 6b      (RowSmall.lean:15, `hB_bounds`, NumCost.lean:13)
```

So e′ is linear in a height h ≈ hB. hB adds up the scales of the weight pattern above: the thick-place weights (≈ b), the cloud (Sc), the η-spread (etaSz) and the t-block (t). *Reading:* this is a lifting exponent that grows linearly with the Hodge–Tate height, as in Fontaine–Laffaille-type bounds, and the height is ≈ b because the weights of the lifts are ≈ b by the size windows. b = exp(x^ε) is the common weight (heights a = x·b, `Scale.a`), chosen exponential so that every polynomial loss is negligible against it (for example etaSz ≤ b/(t³·f·k³), section 16). **b cancels in the balance:** both the main term nT·m·b and the m·B0 = f·m²·b·x^ε cost are linear in b. So e′ ≈ b sets the absolute size of Eb, not its dependence on m.

## 19. The 2/3 counterfactual, computed from the code's own quantities

The outline (entry 14) says a calculation of "the cost of each lift" shows "one would stop at 2/3 a priori". The archive does not contain that calculation (section 18). Here it is reconstructed from the quantities the code actually uses, with the accounting that gives 2/3 named and the code's mechanism that avoids it located.

### What the code charges, per lift (Verified, sections 13 and 18)

```
charge of lift j  =  Σ_{small ℓ} log ℓ · Σ_{pieces i at lift j over ℓ} colength(i)
colength(i)       ≤  (rank)·e′  =  (locDeg·m) · e′          rank from ShapeJumps: |L| = m
e′                ≈  64·hB·sN(ℓ),  hB ∈ [b, 6b]
⟹  charge of lift j  ≈  f·m·b·x^ε  =  B0                    (every lift, the same order)
Eb = Σ_{j=1}^{m} charge(j)  ≈  m·B0
```

Two facts make the charges **add up lift by lift, with nothing counted twice**:

- **Each piece belongs to exactly one lift.** `NData.locs_mem`: i ∈ locs j w ↔ j(i) = j ∧ w(i) = w (section 13).
- **Each lift is charged on its own block.** The family is `DefFam` with `L : J → T → BlockDat` (`Formalism/LocalToGlobal/Divisibility/RowLat.lean:213`), one separate block per lift j. The lift exponents and divisibility lengths of lift j are read from that block alone: `JJ j t w := (F.L j t).JexpU …`, `etaJ j t w := (F.L j t).etaU …` (`LiftLat.lean:147–151`). This matches the outline's picture of each lift adjoining one block, π ↦ π ⊞ μ_j[t] (entry 8). (Verified)

### The balance, per unit of main term (Verified arithmetic)

The main term is nT·m·b, linear in m (m copies of one step's slope, cancelled exactly in `Ga_bound5`). With f = 2n and nT ≈ n:

```
error / main  =  x/m  +  Eb/(nT·m·b)
actual:        Eb ≈ m·B0             ⟹  Eb/(nT·m·b) ≈ f·m·x^ε/nT ≈ 2m·x^ε
              ⟹  x/m + m           minimised at m ≈ √x,       G(x) ≲ x^(1/2 + O(ε))
```

### Accounting 1: charge each lift for the whole chain so far (gives exactly 2/3)

Suppose the divisibility of lift j were measured on the composite Θ_j ∘ … ∘ Θ_1, so that lift j pays again for the blocks of lifts 1, …, j. This is what happens if integrality is re-established after every step rather than tracked along the chain. Then:

```
charge'(j)  =  Σ_{j' ≤ j} charge(j')  ≈  j·B0
Eb'         =  Σ_{j=1}^{m} j·B0  ≈  (m²/2)·B0
Eb'/(nT·m·b)  ≈  m²·x^ε
⟹  x/m + m²     minimised at m = x^(1/3),     G(x) ≲ x^(2/3 + O(ε))
```

**This is the outline's 2/3, exactly.** In the code, the factor m that this accounting adds is removed by the two facts above: pieces are assigned to one lift (`locs_mem`), and each lift's charge is read from its own block (`F.L j t`).

### Accounting 2: no common lattice (no "gcd"); does not give 2/3 in this bookkeeping

Without the glued lattice RR of section 17, each word would need its own target lattice, and the averaging over words in the frame inequality would be lost. The only cost of that averaging visible in the code is the term **log #W** in `Frame_at` (`QuadraticLogDerivCongr.lean:188`), with log #W ≤ (k − 3)·log 2 ≈ x^(9/2+10ε). Against the main term nT·m·b, with b = exp(x^ε), this is negligible. So **in this bookkeeping the gcd does not save a factor of m**. Its role is elsewhere: it makes one divisibility statement hold for all words at once. Whether, without it, divisibility would degrade by a factor of m in some other way is not visible in the code. (Reading)

### Conclusion (Reading, every quantity Verified)

The outline's sentence "precise computation of the cost of each lift, showing that one would stop at 2/3 a priori" is reproduced exactly by **compounding**: charging lift j for everything below it. The archive avoids it because the chain's cost is assigned **piece by piece to exactly one lift, each lift on its own block**, which is the formal content of "tracking divisibility along the chain". What remains per lift is one B0, and its factor m is the Bloch–Kato rank (section 18), so the balance stops at √x and no lower.

**Not established.** That this is the calculation Alpöge did. It is the simplest accounting in the code's own quantities that gives exactly 2/3, and the code's structure is visibly built to avoid it. Accounting 2 is ruled out only as far as the code's visible costs go.

## 20. The degree identity (4c): how a lattice slope becomes "ASK − MAIN"

Issue #5 (§5, §7) flagged as open: "find the lemma or lemmas expressing the lattice degree as ASK plus the period term". **There is no single lemma.** The identity is a chain of four statements, each a field or hypothesis of the family, and all used. Two of them (2 and 3) are where the primes and the periods enter.

### 1. The Arakelov slope of the divisible lattice splits into local indices (Verified)

`NprimeMuR` (`Formalism/SharpRestriction/GlobalTheoremInputs.lean:69`), supplied on the final path through `LinkMu` ⇐ `J.linkMu_of_linkDIV` ⇐ `LinkDIV` (called from `topDesWin`):

```
| μ(Nu u) − μ(NuH u) − Σ_{w ∈ supp} sz(w, sg(u,w)) |  ≤  C·X''
sg(u,w) = Σ_j ( gain_j(w) + F_j(w) − E(u,j,w) )          (DataParams₂.lean: sg)
sz(w,y) = 2·log-weight(w)·e_w·y                           (sz)
```

The slope of the divisible lattice `Nu u` is the slope of a reference lattice `NuH u` plus a sum over finite places of local indices. Summed, these are

```
Ga = Σ_w sz(w, Σ_j gain_j(w) − τ(w)),   Fo = Σ_j Σ_w sz(w, F_j(w)),   Ee(u) = Σ_j Σ_w sz(w, E(u,j,w))
```

which is exactly the combination μmax(M) + Ga + Fo − Ee in the slope bounds of section 15 (`ClaimHigh`, with no error).

### 2. The gain at an inert prime is at least the prime sum (Verified): where ASK enters

`LinkG_at` (`GlobalTheoremInputs.lean:122`), first and third clauses:

```
local divisibility:   DV.LocLe 𝔭_w ⌊ur(τ_w)⌋ (NuLat u) (MramL u)         (the lattice inclusion at w)
at inert w | p ≤ X'':
   Σ_j Σ_t ( vonMangoldtClassSum_{j,t}(p) − loss )  −  m·flG(p)   ≤   Σ_j gain_j(w) − τ(w)
vonMangoldtClassSum_{j,t}(p) = Σ_{i odd} max(0, a_t/p^i − wPart(j,t,p,i))        (DataParams₂.lean)
```

The ask at p is a Legendre-type count: like v_p(a!) = Σ_i ⌊a/p^i⌋, but over odd i and with weights subtracted. And

```
ASK = Σ_j Σ_t ( Σ_{p inert} 2·log p · vonMangoldtClassSum_{j,t}(p)  +  log d · askD … )     (LocalQuadraticParts.lean:470)
```

Summing the clause over the places w | p (sz carries the log-weight, and Σ_{w|p} log-weight·e_w = log p by the product formula, `factEtaMean`, section 9) gives **ASK − Ga ≤ LoP**. That is the `LossBounds` field, proved through `Loss.loss_bound_at` (`PowFourLtCeilThresholdPrime.lean:766`, whose hypothesis `hask` identifies `askAt` with `vonMangoldtClassSum`).

### 3. The dual slope is at most the period main term (Verified): where MAIN enters

`LinkDIV_at` (`GlobalTheoremInputs.lean:77`), the DIV clause:

```
μ_dual(RR) + h(u) − Fo  ≤  MAIN(u) + C·|TT|·a·La + C·m·(B0 + Nc)·x^(Cabs·ε)
MAIN(u) = Σ_t [ m·( phF(a_t) − a_t·logOm ) − a_t·log T_u ]                    (DataParams₂.lean: MAIN)
logOm   = γ + log(π/v) − L′/L(1, χ_d)                                         (DataParams₂.lean: logOm)
```

logOm is the Chowla–Selberg period: `meanPeriod` identifies it with the mean log-period of the CM curves (issue #5 §5; used). phF(a) is the Stirling term a·log a − a (guide §4c). The error terms are the balance of section 14.

### 4. The slope inequality joins the two sides (Verified)

`Frame_at` (`QuadraticLogDerivCongr.lean:188`): the rank-weighted mean s of μ(Nu u) − h_u satisfies s − log #W − (small) ≤ μ_dual(RR), where RR is the glued lattice of section 17.

### Assembled (Reading; each step Verified)

```
ASK − LoP  ≤  Ga                                    (2)  finite places: primes, Legendre counts
μ(Nu u)    ≈  μ(NuH u) + Ga + Fo − Ee(u)            (1)  Arakelov degree = reference + local indices
mean_u [ μ(Nu u) − h_u ]  ≤  μ_dual(RR) + log #W    (4)  Bost slope inequality, one common lattice
μ_dual(RR) + h(u) − Fo   ≤  MAIN(u) + errors        (3)  infinite places: periods (Chowla–Selberg)
⟹  ASK − MAIN  ≤  errors   (after the m·sumA·lg main terms cancel in Ga_bound5, section 6)
```

## 25. Closing the three gaps, and how the final theorem is assembled

### 25.1 From an upper bound on windows to "no zeros" (closes the gap in 23)

**Exact.** The last analytic step is `zeroFree_of_rpow_window` (LandauTheorems/ZeroFreeOfRpowWindow.lean:11): for a `CharFamily` F (finitely many Dirichlet characters χ_i with weights a_i > 0, trivial character only at modulus 1),

  if for every Y0 there is Y ≥ Y0 with Gm F(y) ≤ C·y^θ for all y in [Y, Y^(1+δ)], then no L(s, χ_i) vanishes for Re s > θ.

Here Gm F = Σ a_i · Re Gχ_i, and Gχ(y) = Σ_n Λ(n)χ(n)(y/n − 1)₊ minus its main term (y log y − (1+γ)y for the trivial character, −y·L′/L(1, χ) otherwise). It goes through `lemmaL` and `landau_window`, whose proof splits into `landau_window_key`, `landau_dichotomy` and `landau_nearest`; the pole data comes from `poleData` (the Mellin transform has its poles at the zeros of the L(s, χ_i) and of their conjugates). All used.

**Reading.** This is a windowed version of Landau's theorem. The classical one needs a one-sided bound for *all* large y; here it is only needed on windows [Y, Y^(1+δ)] that reach arbitrarily far out. `VWinAt` (section 24) is stated for x in [Y, Y²] with Y ≥ X^(1/J), and `supplyChi` turns it into `HostExW (3 / 2)`, so the Landau step is applied with δ = 1/2, on windows [Y, Y^(3/2)] (correction, after Manvir Jaswal's guide). The whole proof only has to work on such windows. It also explains why one sign is enough: the weights a_i are positive and each character comes with its conjugate, so an upper bound on Gm controls the poles.

**Exact (top of the proof).** `Main.lean`: `riemannHypothesis := rhOfAll`, `rhOfAll := rh_of_every allChi`, and `allChi := FinalTheorems.everyCharacter : EveryCharacter`, which says L(s, χ) ≠ 0 for every Dirichlet character χ and every Re s > 1/2 (except the pole at s = 1). `everyCharacter := everyCharacter_of_row galoisLocalGlobalStrong_recParameter`, which combines the literature-level inputs with `VWin` via `vwin_of_row`. So the Lean theorem proves GRH for all Dirichlet L-functions and gets RH as the trivial-character case.

## 26. Closing the open questions (20 Nov, 23:30 local)

**Q1, is compounding the outline's 2/3? Still open.** The outline's entry 14 (dated 15 Nov) reports a computation of the cost of each lift and the figure 2/3, but does not show the calculation. Section 19 stays a reconstruction.

**Q2, can a smaller indefinite part per lift lower the m² of Eb? Answered within this chain.** `ShapeJumps k m t` is required with the same m at every local piece, whatever its lift (`admG2`, `EmbeddingTypes.lean:122`, Verified). So every lift is charged rank m. Even a rank that tracked the shrinking indefinite part, m − j at lift j, would pay Σ_j (m − j) = m(m−1)/2, still of order m² (Reading, arithmetic). At most a factor 2 is available; lowering the exponent needs rank o(m) per lift, i.e. a different, shorter chain, which costs on the x/m side.

**Q3, why is e′ ≈ b = exp(x^ε)? Answered within this design.** e′ is linear in the Hodge–Tate height hB ∈ [b, 6b] (section 18). The weight scale cannot be polynomial because the Ш-window losses are exponential themselves: `BaAt` uses exp(x^(4ε/5)) and `HVAt` (cond·t)^C + C·exp(x^(4ε/5)), both absorbed by √b = exp(x^ε/2) (Exact, definitions). b cancels in the balance, so this fixes the size of Eb, not the exponent.

**Q4, do the formal classical theorems match the literature? Two of four.**
- Ferrero–Washington (Exact): |Cl(K′)/Cl(K′)^ℓ| ≤ ℓ^r for all K′ = K(μ_{ℓ^n}), K abelian with μ_ℓ ⊆ K (μ_4 if ℓ = 2), Mathlib's `ClassGroup`. Reading: bounded ℓ-rank up the cyclotomic Z_ℓ-tower, equivalent to μ = 0.
- Poitou–Tate (Exact): `PoitouTateNormalised`: number field F, ℓ ≠ 2 or F totally complex, S ⊇ places above ℓ, every finite Z/ℓ^r G_S-module: normalised local invariants, the nine-term exact sequence, Ш-duality, H^n = 0 for n ≥ 3. **Q5, is the windowed Landau theorem new? Narrowed.** Zero-free regions from one-sided arithmetic information is the Landau–Pintz line (survey: "A round of Pintz to celebrate oscillations in sums", arXiv:2511.09978). Close to known methods; the exact form is for specialists.
