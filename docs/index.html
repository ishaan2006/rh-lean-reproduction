1 Introduction

1.1 What is proved

Theorem 1 (Main theorem, UpperTheory.EveryCharacter). For every modulus \(N\ge 1\), every Dirichlet character \(\chi \bmod N\) and every \(s\in\mathbb{C}\) with \(\operatorname{Re}s > 1/2\), if \(\chi\neq 1\) or \(s\neq 1\) then \(L(s,\chi)\neq 0\).

The Riemann Hypothesis is the trivial-character case. In the Lean source: \[\begin{gathered} \texttt{riemannHypothesis} := \texttt{rhOfAll},\qquad \texttt{rhOfAll} := \texttt{rh}\_\texttt{of}\_\texttt{every allChi},\\ \texttt{allChi} := \texttt{FinalTheorems.everyCharacter}. \end{gathered}\] Exact (files Main.lean, MainTheorems.lean and MainConclusion.lean).

1.2 What this document is, and is not

1.3 The idea in one paragraph

Fix a character \(\chi\) and a large \(x\). The proof attaches to \(\chi\), and to two auxiliary primes \(q\equiv 3 \pmod 8\), \(d\equiv 7\pmod{16}\), a family of arithmetic objects: Hermitian lattices indexed by words \(u\), carried along a chain of \(m\) theta lifts between unitary groups whose signature moves from \((k-1,m)\) to \((k+m-1,0)\), one step per lift. One quantity, \(\mathrm{ASK}\), is computed twice. At the finite places it is a sum over primes of Legendre-type counts weighted by \(\chi\); at the infinite places it is controlled by Chowla–Selberg periods, giving a main term \(\mathrm{MAIN}\). A slope inequality for one common lattice joins the two and yields \(\mathrm{ASK}\le \mathrm{MAIN}+ \mathrm{Err}\), with \(\mathrm{Err}/(\text{main}) \approx x/m + m\). With \(m\approx\sqrt{x}\) this gives a bound of size \(x^{1/2+O(\varepsilon)}\) for a weighted prime sum. Fourier analysis on a small group isolates the prime sum of \(\chi\), and a windowed Landau theorem converts the one-sided bound into zero-freeness.

The author’s outline [1] describes the chain of lifts as playing the role of multiplicity in Stepanov’s method [5], and says that charging the lifts one by one would stop at exponent \(2/3\). Section 9 reconstructs that \(2/3\) from the code’s own quantities and shows how the code avoids it.

1.4 Structure of the proof

  1. Windowed Landau (Section 3). A one-sided bound on windows \([Y,Y^{1+\delta}]\) rules out zeros.

  2. Fourier isolation (Section 4). Averaging over a thick set isolates the prime sum of the target character; the auxiliary characters are made harmless by choosing \(q,d\) with a zero-density estimate.

  3. The size bound (Section 5). The error relative to the main term is about \(x/m+m\).

  4. The divisibility layer (Section 6). The degree identity, the common lattice, the rank bound and local divisibility.

  5. Near-orthogonality (Section 7). The words are orthogonal up to \(\exp(-x^{\varepsilon/8})\).

  6. The \(\text{Ш}\) window (Section 8). Tate–Shafarevich losses over cyclotomic layers of degree about \(x^2\), resting on formal Iwasawa theory.

3 Step A: from a bound on windows to the absence of zeros

3.1 The quantity bounded

Definition 2 (Deduction.Gchi, Formalism.Gz). For \(y\ge 1\) let \(\operatorname{ramp}_\chi(y)=\sum_{n\le y}\Lambda(n)\chi(n)\,(y/n-1)\). Put \[G_\chi(y) = \begin{cases} \operatorname{ramp}_\chi(y) - y\log y + (1+\gamma)y, & \chi = 1,\\[2pt] \operatorname{ramp}_\chi(y) + y\,\dfrac{L'(1,\chi)}{L(1,\chi)}, & \chi\neq 1. \end{cases}\] For \(\chi=1\) this is \(G_\zeta(x) = \sum_{n\le x}\Lambda(n)(x/n-1) - x\log x + (1+\gamma)x\) (Formalism/StatementOC.lean:13).

\(\operatorname{ramp}_\chi(y) = \int_1^y \psi(t,\chi)\,dt/t\) is the logarithmic Riesz mean of the Chebyshev function, and \(G_\chi\) is its remainder after the main term. RH is equivalent to \(G_\zeta(x) = O(x^{1/2+\varepsilon})\).

Remark 3 (Verified). In one place in the development the von Mangoldt function appears under the name abelian_2, defined as ArithmeticFunction.vonMangoldt. Searches for the usual names miss it; Step 5 of the author’s timeline (“definitively switch to \(\Lambda\)”) is exactly the expression \(G_\zeta\) above.

3.2 The windowed Landau theorem

Definition 4 (CharFamily). A character family is a finite set of Dirichlet characters \(\chi_i \bmod N_i\) with weights \(a_i>0\), the trivial character occurring only with modulus \(1\). Put \(G_m(y) = \sum_i a_i \operatorname{Re}G_{\chi_i}(y)\).

Theorem 5 (Windowed Landau, zeroFree_of_rpow_window, Exact). Let \(0<\theta<1\), \(\delta>0\), \(C\) real. Suppose that for every \(Y_0\) there is \(Y\ge Y_0\) with \[G_m(y)\le C\,y^{\theta}\qquad\text{for all } y\in[Y,\,Y^{1+\delta}].\] Then \(L(s,\chi_i)\neq 0\) for every \(i\) and every \(\operatorname{Re}s>\theta\).

Proof sketch (from the Lean proof). The proof goes through lemmaL and landau_window. poleData builds a function \(\Phi\) (the Mellin transform of \(G_m\), assembled from the characters and their inverses) whose poles in \(\operatorname{Re}s>0\) are exactly the zeros of the \(L(s,\chi_i)\) and of their conjugates, and shows the zeros are isolated and have real part at most \(2\) (landau_pole_re_le). Suppose a zero \(\rho_0\) has \(\operatorname{Re}\rho_0>\theta\). landau_window_key produces, from the window hypothesis (in the integrated form WindowHypU), a real \(\sigma\) and a growth bound for the Mellin integral restricted to windows. landau_dichotomy then splits into two cases: either there is a zero-free disc around a real point with a zero on its boundary, or there is an isolated real zero \(\beta\) in the region; landau_nearest picks the nearest pole. In both cases the window bound contradicts the growth forced by the pole (landau_growth_contra, landau_window_contra). ◻

Remark 6 (Why one sign is enough, Reading checked by arithmetic). In the levels actually used, Level.cyc psi is the cyclic group generated by a primitive \(\psi\), each of its \(o-1\) nontrivial members weighted \(1/(3o+1)\). Every Dirichlet coefficient of the combined series is then at least \(\Lambda(n)\bigl(1-\tfrac{o-1}{3o+1}\bigr)>0\). Positive coefficients are exactly what Landau’s argument needs, so an upper bound on \(G_m\) alone forces zero-freeness. The classical theorem needs the bound for all large \(y\); here a sequence of windows reaching to infinity suffices.

Remark 7 (Verified). A different Landau theorem, zero_free_of_Chebyshev_growth in the namespace Formalism.HC.Landau, is proved in the archive but not used.

4 Step B: isolating the target character

4.1 The group and its characters

The host comes with a finite abelian group \(H\) and a group \(E_0\) with four characters \(0,\psi,\chi,\chi\psi\) (psC, chC, chpsC). Exact (CharacterSignsAtBaseClass) At the base class \(g_0\): \[\chi(g_0)=-1,\qquad \psi(g_0)=+1,\qquad \chi\psi(g_0)=-1.\] Define \[G_{\mathrm{sig}}= G_0 + G_\psi - G_\chi - G_{\chi\psi}.\] This is the Fourier projection onto the class of \(g_0\); its trivial part \(G_0\) carries the prime sum of the target.

4.2 An exact identity

Proposition 10 (Deduction.ch_balance, Exact). For every \(a>0\), \(t\in H\), weights \(w>0\) on the admissible set and \(N\) large enough, \[\mathrm{ASK}(D,N,a,t,w) - \mathrm{MAIN}(\ell,a,w) = \operatorname{De}(D,\ell,a,t,w),\] where \(\operatorname{De}\) is an explicit combination of the \(G_\psi\) evaluated at \(a/w\).

This is an identity, not an inequality: it expands the counted quantity \(\mathrm{ASK}\) over all characters of \(E_0\times H\) (askExp). Averaging over the thick set collapses the character sum.

Proposition 11 (ThickPartTranslateMean, Exact). For a thick set \(T_2\) of density \(\delta_2 = |T_2|/|H|\), \[\frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{u_0\in H}\frac{1}{|\Phi|}\sum_{\substack{g\notin\Phi\\ g \text{ thick at } u_0}} \sum_{\psi}\overline{\psi(g)}\, b\,G_\psi(A/b) \;=\; \frac{\delta_2\, b}{2}\,G_{\mathrm{sig}}(A/b).\]

4.3 The growth inequality

Theorem 12 (GrowthInequalityExplicit, Exact). Under GrowthInequalityHypotheses (which include \(\mathrm{topExcess}\le \mathrm{Err}\) and ValuesAtX), \[|T_T|\, m\, b\,\frac{\delta_2}{2}\Bigl(G_\zeta(x) - 3x^{1/2+\varepsilon} - \mathrm{moveCost}\Bigr) \le \mathrm{Err}+ |T_T|\, m\cdot 2f_{Pl}(x+1)\,b\,\theta + |T_T|\, m\cdot 6f_{Pl}(x+1)(\eta+1),\] where \(\mathrm{topExcess} = \frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{u_0}\sum_{t\in T_T}\sum_{j}\bigl(\mathrm{ASK}- \operatorname{Re}\mathrm{MAIN}\bigr)\) and \(\mathrm{moveCost} = C_d + 3(\log(x+1)+C_d+C_L\log x)\).

The term \(3x^{1/2+\varepsilon}\) comes from signal_term: for \(|y-x|\le 1\), \[G_\zeta(x) - 3x^{1/2+\varepsilon} - \mathrm{moveCost} \le \operatorname{Re}G_{\mathrm{sig}}(y),\] using ValuesAtX: \(\|G_\psi(x)\|\le x^{1/2+\varepsilon}\) and \(\|\ell(\psi)\|\le C_L\log x\) for the three nontrivial characters of \(E_0\), and a Lipschitz bound (DerivInt).

Remark 13 (Reading). Dividing Theorem 12 by \(|T_T|\,m\,b\,\delta_2/2\) gives \[G_\zeta(x)\le 3x^{1/2+\varepsilon} + \frac{\mathrm{Err}}{|T_T|\,m\,b\,\delta_2/2}+\dots\] So the whole problem is to prove \(\mathrm{Err}\lesssim |T_T|\,m\,b\,x^{1/2+O(\varepsilon)}\). Sections 5 and 6 do exactly that.

4.4 The auxiliary characters are chosen, not controlled

The characters \(\psi,\chi,\chi\psi\) are twists by the quadratic characters of the auxiliary primes \(q\equiv 3\pmod 8\) and \(d\equiv 7\pmod{16}\) (PrimePair). Their bounds in ValuesAtX come from the following chain, all Exact: QH.pairEx \(\leftarrow\) pair_exists \(\leftarrow\cdots\leftarrow\) count_bad_core \(\leftarrow\) ZD.zero_density_box.

Definition 14 (Box). For a character \(\psi\), \(\mathrm{Box}(Q,C,\tau,Y,X,\psi)\) means: \(\psi\) has a good conductor, \(\|L'/L(1,\psi)\|\le C\log Y\), and \(\|G_\psi(y)\|\le y^{1/2+\tau}\) for all \(y\in[Y,X]\).

Theorem 15 (count_bad_core, Exact). There is \(C\ge 0\) such that for \(Q\ge 3\) and any set \(S\) of primes \(q>F\) each carrying a primitive character of conductor \(fq\le Q\), \(f\le F\), without a zero-free box, \(|S|\le C\,Q^{1/4}\).

The zero-density estimate zero_density_box (compare [5, Ch. 10]) is assembled from a coefficient square bound, zero detection by a mollifier identity, a duality inequality and a pair-twist bound.

Remark 16 (Reading). The auxiliary \(L\)-functions are not proved to satisfy GRH. The proof picks \(q\) and \(d\) outside the few bad moduli. The target character is the only one whose \(L\)-function is not controlled in advance.

5 Step C: the size bound, where \(\sqrt{x}\) is paid

5.2.0.1 Why \(n\approx x^2\) and \(k\approx x^{9/2}\): the word count. Ours

The one arithmetic link between \(m\) and \(k\) is the number of words. Verified: \(\log\Gamma = C_\Gamma\, m^5 f L\) (gamB, RowsConc.lean:52), the number of words is \(\lceil 3\Gamma^2x^{C_F}\rceil\), words are coded injectively by \(k-2\) bits (exists_injective_words), and Win_at requires \(\log|W|\le(k-3)\log 2\). 9 Why the \(2/3\) barrier does not appear

The outline (entry 14) says a calculation of the cost of each lift shows one would “stop at \(2/3\) a priori”. That calculation is not in the archive (Section 9.4). Here it is reconstructed from the code’s own quantities. Ours.

9.1 What the code charges

Verified \[\mathrm{charge}(j)=\sum_{\ell\text{ small}}\log\ell\sum_{\text{pieces at }j}\mathrm{colength} \approx f\,m\,b\,x^\varepsilon=B_0,\qquad E_b=\sum_{j=1}^m\mathrm{charge}(j)\approx m B_0.\] Each piece belongs to one lift (NData.locs_mem), and each lift is charged on its own block: the family is DefFam with \(L:J\to T\to\mathrm{BlockDat}\) (RowLat.lean:213), and the exponents and lengths of lift \(j\) are read from \(L(j,t)\) alone (LiftLat.lean:147--151).

9.2 Accounting 1: compounding gives exactly \(2/3\)

If lift \(j\) were charged for the whole composite \(\Theta_j\circ\dots\circ\Theta_1\), then \[\mathrm{charge}'(j)=\sum_{j'\le j}\mathrm{charge}(j')\approx jB_0,\qquad E_b'=\sum_{j=1}^m jB_0\approx\frac{m^2}{2}B_0,\qquad \frac{E_b'}{n_Tmb}\approx m^2x^\varepsilon,\] and the balance becomes \(x/m+m^2\), minimised at \(m=x^{1/3}\), giving \(x^{2/3}\). This is the outline’s \(2/3\) exactly. The two facts above remove the extra factor of \(m\).

9.3 Accounting 2: no gcd does not give \(2/3\)

Without the common lattice, the averaging over words in Frame_at would be lost. Its only visible cost is \(\log\#W\le(k-3)\log2\approx x^{9/2+10\varepsilon}\), negligible against \(n_Tmb\). So in this bookkeeping the gcd does not save a factor of \(m\); its role is to make one divisibility statement hold for all words.

9.4 No \(2/3\) route in the archive

Exact About twenty unused variants of the final growth bound exist, among them:

  • ChainAt, Chain6At, ChainAt7;

  • FixedEpsilonGrowthBound;

  • GrowthBracketBoundAtPoint;

  • PowerBoundGivesRiemannHypothesis.

Every one concludes \(G(x)\le x^{1/2+O(\varepsilon)}\). No \(m^2B_0\) budget, no per-word or per-character dual lattice, and no balance with exponent \(2/3\) or \(1/3\) appears anywhere. The only “\(2/3\)” constants are factors like \((2/3)^f\) in volume bounds.

9.5 A planned test

We designed a perturbation experiment (mechanism/PERTURB.md): loosen only the upper bound on \(m\) in RankVal by \(x^{1/40}\), rebuild the roughly 13,000 dependent modules, and record which proofs fail. Our prediction, written before running it: the failures appear in the window lemmas (szRow_rankSizes, winA) and in the \(m^5\) word-count lemma, not in the arithmetic layers, because \(m\) and \(k\) are locked with only about \(x^{0.26\varepsilon}\) of headroom. The results will be posted as an update to this document.

11 Open questions, and what we could settle

Each question below is followed by what we could settle from the source and the outline. Status: four answered within the code (2, 3, 4, 6), one narrowed (5), one still needs the author (1); the call-level gap in step D4, the role of the constants and a static reading of SizesAt are settled below.

Question 1. Is the compounding counterfactual of Section 9 the calculation behind the outline’s \(2/3\)?

Still open. The outline (entry 14, dated 15 November 2026) reports a “precise computation of the cost of each lift” and the figure \(2/3\), but does not show the calculation. Our reconstruction is the simplest accounting in the code’s own quantities that gives exactly \(2/3\); only the author can say whether it is his.

Question 2. Could a family with a smaller indefinite part per lift lower the \(m^2\) cost of \(E_b\), or does the Bloch–Kato count force it?

Answered within this chain. Verified ShapeJumps k m t is required with the same \(m\) at every local piece, whatever lift the piece belongs to (admG2, EmbeddingTypes.lean:122), so every lift is charged rank \(m\). Reading checked by arithmetic: even a family whose rank tracked the shrinking indefinite part, \(m-j\) at lift \(j\), would pay \(\sum_{j=1}^{m}(m-j)=m(m-1)/2\), still of order \(m^2\). So no refinement of the per-lift rank along this chain changes the exponent; it saves at most a factor of \(2\). Lowering it would need a rank of \(o(m)\) per lift, that is a chain with a smaller indefinite part, hence fewer lifts, which costs on the \(x/m\) side.

Question 3. Why must the lift exponent \(e'\) be as large as \(b=\exp(x^\varepsilon)\)? A polynomial \(e'\) would shrink \(B_0\), though not its dependence on \(m\).

Answered within this design. Verified \(e'\) is linear in a Hodge–Tate height \(h_B\in[b,6b]\), and the heights are of size \(b\) because the weights on thick places lie in \([b,b+\eta+1]\). Exact The weight scale cannot be polynomial: the \(\text{Ш}\)-window losses are themselves exponential. The bounded-height condition is stated with \(\exp(x^{4\varepsilon/5})\) (BaAt) and the plus part with \((\mathrm{cond}\cdot t)^C+C\exp(x^{4\varepsilon/5})\) (HVAt), both to be absorbed by \(\sqrt b=\exp(x^\varepsilon/2)\). Reading So \(b\) is exponential to swallow those losses, \(e'\approx b\) follows, and since \(b\) cancels in the balance this sets the absolute size of \(E_b\) but not the exponent \(1/2\).

Question 4. Do the formal versions of Ferrero–Washington, Poitou–Tate, the Kato-type bounds and the CM inputs match the published theorems at full strength?

Answered: statements read for all four.

  • Ferrero–Washington. Exact The statement bounds \(|\operatorname{Cl}(K')/\operatorname{Cl}(K')^\ell|\) by \(\ell^r\) for every layer \(K'=K(\mu_{\ell^n})\) over an abelian \(K\supseteq\mu_\ell\) (\(\mu_4\) if \(\ell=2\)), with Mathlib’s class group. Reading \(\operatorname{Cl}/\operatorname{Cl}^\ell\) is elementary abelian of order \(\ell^{\operatorname{rk}_\ell}\), so this is bounded \(\ell\)-rank up the cyclotomic \(\mathbb{Z}_\ell\)-tower, which is equivalent to \(\mu=0\): the published theorem, in the form for \(K\) containing \(\mu_\ell\).

  • Poitou–Tate. Exact PoitouTateNormalised: for a number field \(F\), a prime \(\ell\) with \(\ell\neq2\) or \(F\) totally complex, a finite set \(S\) containing the places above \(\ell\), and every finite \(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^r\)-module with a continuous \(G_S\)-action: normalised local invariant maps, the nine-term exact sequence, \(\text{Ш}\)-duality, and \(H^n=0\) for \(n\ge3\). Reading This is the standard theorem; the condition on \(\ell\) is exactly the case where the real places contribute nothing in degree \(\ge3\). It is proved unconditionally by poitouTateNormalised_holds; the literature wrapper ignores its argument.

  • Kato-type input. Exact KatoTwoW: for an odd prime \(\ell\), an imaginary quadratic \(K\) with \(\ell\nmid \mathrm{disc}(K)\,h_K\), and a two-dimensional \(\ell\)-adic representation \(W\) with determinant the quadratic character \(\theta\) of \(K\), ordinary at \(\ell\), residually Eisenstein (trace \(\equiv\theta\cdot\chi^e\psi+(\chi^e\psi)^{-1}\)), irreducible over the cyclotomic extension of \(K\) and attached to a newform of weight \(2e+1\): the Bloch–Kato Selmer groups of \(W\) and of \(W\otimes\theta\) vanish. Reading As David Loeffler pointed out on Zulip (21 November 2026), this matches Kato’s theorem [8] directly without needing an explicit \(L\)-value hypothesis: in this setting the relevant critical \(L\)-value sits at \(s = 1\) in the analytic normalisation (to the right of the central point \(s = 1/2\)), where non-vanishing is automatic. It is proved in Lean (katoTwoW_of_printsH_of_rT_of_quadBC2).

  • CM inputs. Exact Read statement by statement: Shimura’s main theorem of complex multiplication for imaginary quadratic \(K\) (EllipticMainTheoremCM); \(K(j(\mathcal{O}_K))\) is the Hilbert class field, of degree \(h_K\), with \(j(\mathfrak a)=j(\mathfrak b)\) iff \(\mathfrak a,\mathfrak b\) are in the same class (HilbertClassFieldJ); Siegel–Klingen and Deligne–Ribet; the functional equation of partial zeta functions of totally real fields and of Hecke characters of CM fields; Grothendieck–Messing deformation theory over nilpotent divided powers; de Rham cohomology of abelian schemes (free, Hodge filtration, base change, rigidity, duality); the period relation and Gauss–Manin periods; and Serre–Tate (Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich, potential good reduction, good reduction of CM abelian varieties). Reading Each matches the standard theorem of that name in shape and hypotheses. We did not check the internal definitions they rely on (for example the global Artin map) line by line.

Since every one of these is proved in Lean and the axiom check is clean, Question 4 is about exposition: whether the names are honest descriptions. For the ones we read they are, with the caveat on the Kato-type statement above.

Question 5. Is the windowed Landau theorem (Theorem 5) new as a stated result?

Narrowed. Deducing zero-free regions from one-sided or localised information on prime sums is a classical line going back to Landau [9] and developed by Pintz (see the recent survey [7]), and effective versions of Landau’s theorem already give quantitative lower bounds for averages over ranges \([1,Y]\). The windowed statement is therefore close to known methods in substance; whether this exact form appears in the literature is for specialists to say.

Question 6. Does the formal proof require Grothendieck universes, or does it hold in standard ZFC?

Answered within standard foundations. Verified The mathematical statements (riemannHypothesis, GRH) live entirely in Type 0 (second-order arithmetic). In the published 61.6 GB export, an automated level census (analysis/levels.py) finds 21,604 level records, with concrete usage: 369,334 at Sort 0 (Prop), 36,070 at Sort 1 (Type 0), 3,868 at Sort 2 (Type 1), 100 at Sort 3 (Type 2), and exactly 4 occurrences of Level 4 (all polymorphic constants Exists, Exists.intro, inferInstance over Type 2, with 0 Sort 4 occurrences). Reading As Joël Riou pointed out on Zulip (November 2026), the presence of Type 2 is an artifact of Mathlib's generic category theory instantiating sheaves over "big" étale sites \(\mathrm{Sch}_{/S}\). For any scheme \(X\) of finite type over a number field, the small étale site \(X_\mathrm{et}\) (consisting of étale schemes of finite presentation over \(X\)) has at most countably many isomorphism classes (\(|X_\mathrm{et}|\le\aleph_0\)), so \(X_\mathrm{et}\) has a skeleton in Type 0. Sheaves on \(X_\mathrm{et}\) form a locally small category with morphisms in Type 0, derived categories have small hom-sets, and all cohomology groups, crystalline/de Rham deformations (Grothendieck-Messing), Galois cohomology, Bloch-Kato Selmer groups, and class groups (Ferrero-Washington) live strictly in Type 0. The formal proof is therefore conservative over standard ZFC without inaccessible cardinals.

11.0.0.3 The constants do not matter for the theorem.

Exact ChebyshevInequalityAtWindowScale asks for some constants \(e_D,e_L\) and \(\varepsilon_1>0\) such that for every \(\varepsilon<\varepsilon_1\) there is a family with \(G_m(x)\le x^{1/2+(e_D+e_L+7)\varepsilon}\) on its windows. Since \(e_D,e_L\) are fixed while \(\varepsilon\to0\), the Landau step gives zero-freeness for every \(\operatorname{Re}s>1/2\) whatever their values. Explicit constants would only matter for an effective (numerical) version, which the development does not attempt.

11.0.0.4 SizesAt under other exponents, read statically.

Exact Write \(m=x^\mu\), \(n=x^\nu\), \(k=x^\kappa\). In the abstract size layer the exponents enter only through \[\tfrac12-\varepsilon\le\mu\le\tfrac12+\tfrac\varepsilon 2,\qquad \kappa\ge1+\mu,\qquad \kappa\le 2\nu+\tfrac12-\varepsilon,\qquad \kappa\le e_K\] (the bounds on \(\mu\) are in RestSize and ErrWindows0), together with the family’s word count \(\kappa\ge5\mu+\nu\). Every other window (CostWindows, GrowthInequalitySizes, the bounds on \(L\), \(L_a\), \(b'\)) compares polynomials in \(x\) with \(b=\exp(x^\varepsilon)\) and holds for any fixed exponents. So statically, \(\mu\) is pinned only by the two windows that encode the balance, exactly as predicted. What reading cannot rule out is a hidden dependence elsewhere in the 13,000 modules downstream of the size choices; the perturbation run (Section 9) will check that mechanically.
